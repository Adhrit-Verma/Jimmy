An always-on assistant for Windows

Remembers what you saw. Speaks only when asked.

Jimmy turns your screen's text and the speech near your mic into one searchable history, kept on your own PC. Say "Jimmy, …" and it answers out loud, beside the moments it found. The rest of the time, it stays quiet.

The idea

Capturing everything is easy. Staying quiet is the product.

Jimmy is built for six good interruptions an evening, not a stream of notifications. A model never writes a card: code writes every line from words in your own data, with real times.

10min between two cardsNever two in a row, however much changes.

Ask

Say it the way you remember it

No keywords and no app to open. Jimmy searches by meaning inside the time you name, shows the moments it used, and answers out loud in about a second. After it answers, keep talking for ten seconds without the name.

  • what was that fellowship application I saw on Tuesday?Searches Tuesday by keywords and meaning; evidence on the left, answer on the right.
  • how long was I on YouTube today?Instant, with no model: one line written by code, plus a chart.
  • remind me at 5 to call SamA card and a spoken reminder at five.
  • forget the last hourSays exactly what would go, then waits for your "yes".

Hands

Its own cursor, never yours

Ask it to do something on screen and Jimmy makes a short plan, plays it back live, and presses and types through Windows' accessibility interface. Every action is checked before the next. A policy in code decides what it may do, whatever model proposed it:

  • RefuseTyping into a password box, or words you didn't say.
  • StopA CAPTCHA. Jimmy never solves one.
  • AskAnything that can't be undone, or a site you didn't name.
  • AllowAn undoable action on a control you named, shown first, with a moment to say "stop".

Get started

One setup file, then Jimmy sets itself up

Download Jimmy-Setup-1.0.0.exe (about 170 MB) from the project's Releases page on GitHub. It installs for you alone, on any drive, with no Python, Node or admin rights needed. On first start Jimmy checks your PC, asks where to keep your data and how much of the PC it may use, then fetches its own Python and speech model.

  • Windows 10 (22H2) or 11, 64-bit
  • Lite ~2.5 GB
  • Balanced ~4.5 GB
  • Full ~11 GB, with local AI

The setup isn't code-signed yet, so Windows shows "Windows protected your PC": choose More info, then Run anyway. NVIDIA GPUs get fast GPU speech; other PCs use the processor.

Questions

Before you install it

Does Jimmy send my screen to the cloud?

Not continuously. Captures are stored on your PC. When you ask a question, the evidence shown with the answer (at most 6,000 characters) goes to the model provider you chose. A question about your screen also sends that window's latest picture, with faces already blurred. Without a key, nothing is sent. The full list.

Does it work without an API key?

Yes. Capture, search, the timeline, insights and cards all run locally. Without a key, each answer shows what search found (exactly what would have been sent to the model) instead of a spoken reply.

Which model providers does it support?

NVIDIA (the default; a free key from build.nvidia.com), Google Gemini on its free tier with NVIDIA as a backup, or OpenAI (paid). Card decisions and meaning search always run locally through Ollama.

Does it record other people?

The mic hears the room, so speech near it is transcribed. System audio, such as the other side of a call, is never captured. Faces in screenshots are blurred, only counts are kept, and the webcam recognises only you, and only after you say "remember my face".

Can it click and type on its own?

Only when you asked, it is sure which control you meant, and the action can be undone. It shows its cursor first and gives you a moment to say "stop". Anything that can't be undone, or a site you didn't name, waits for your "yes". It never types into a password box or acts in banking or password-manager windows.

Will it run on my PC?

Windows 10 (22H2) or 11, 64-bit. An NVIDIA GPU makes speech recognition fast, but it isn't required: other PCs use the processor. Jimmy was built and measured on a laptop with a 6 GB RTX 4050.

Which languages does it understand?

English and Hindi. Hindi works through one model call that also returns the English; its real-world accuracy has not been measured yet.

Is it open source?

Yes, MIT licensed. The code, every design decision and the evidence behind it are public (View on GitHub, at the top of this page).
